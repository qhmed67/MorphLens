## 2. GPU verification

The brief requires the NVIDIA card, not the CPU.

- **Rendering:** the headless Chromium is launched with `--gl=egl`
  (`Config.setChromiumOpenGlRenderer('egl')`); on this machine EGL resolves to
  `libEGL_nvidia.so.580.178.04`, so rasterisation and compositing happen on the
  RTX 2050. `nvidia-smi` shows the GPU busy while a render is in flight.
- **Encoding:** the render log prints
  `[stitchFramesToVideo()] Encoder: h264_nvenc, hardware accelerated: true`,
  i.e. Remotion's bundled FFmpeg used the NVENC encoder.
- Remotion disables the hardware encoder when `--crf` is set, so quality is
  controlled with `Config.setVideoBitrate('12M')` instead. **Do not re-add
  `Config.setCrf(...)`** — it silently falls back to `libx264` on the CPU.
- Wall-clock for the full 1808-frame render: **≈31 s** (6 worker processes).

## 2b. Audio verification

- The exported audio was decoded to PCM and measured: **peak −10.5 dBFS, zero
  clipped samples**, RMS −37 dBFS (a sparse, percussive mix, as expected with
  no music and no narration).
- The 100 ms envelope was scanned for sound events: **22 distinct motion
  sounds**, each landing on the frame of the movement it belongs to
  (0.00 s push-in · 2.60 s the 16×16 mark · 3.80 s the window opens ·
  10.30 s the cell highlight · 16.60 / 17.80 s the value and the result ·
  19.30 s the photo changes · 28.70 s the curve is drawn ·
  40.30 / 41.70 / 43.20 s the three sharpening stages · 44.90 / 45.80 s the
  arithmetic · 51.90 s the end card).
- Every SFX file is peak-normalised to −0.7 dBFS by the generator, and the
  generator **throws** if a sound comes out silent or contains a non-finite
  sample. (This caught a real bug: a typed-array multiplication made the
  `sweep` sound digital silence, so the three stage sounds were missing.)

1155 frames / 38.51 s / ≈5.4 Mbps (25.8 MB), AAC 48 kHz stereo.

- **Audio:** peak −9.8 dBFS, **0 clipped samples**.
- **Frames:** 154 frames sampled at 4 fps — **no black frames**; the only

- **The effects:** all twenty are implemented in `src/effects/index.tsx` and

## 4. Visual checks performed

Frames were extracted from the delivered MP4 with FFmpeg and inspected at
t = 0.5, 4.2, 4.6, 8, 13, 18, 22, 27, 31, 36, 40, 45, 50, 55, 60 s:

- **Hook (0–4.5 s):** the photo pushes in, the 16×16 window is marked, the
  caption and the mono card land inside the safe area.
- **Pixel window:** every cell shows a legible red-channel value; the ink colour
  flips per cell (dark ink on light pixels, paper on dark pixels) so the numbers
  stay readable on both the sky and the ant's leg; the address row is sparse
  (`406, 410, 414, 418 …`) and the highlighted cell carries its `(412, 455)` tag.
- **Brightness:** the arithmetic appears before the photo changes; the slider
  runs 0 → +40 in step with the cross-fade to the real `brightness(+40)` frame;
  the histogram morphs with it.
- **Contrast:** the transfer curve draws live (c 1.00 → 1.30) against the
  dashed identity diagonal; the operating point and the formula card stay
  inside the panel.
- **Sharpen:** the four stages cross-fade through one shared stage position
  (the app's own Trace rule), the 3×3 kernel window slides over the magnified
  pixels, and the maths card is only shown once its content is on screen.
- **End card:** the app's editor chrome, the *Maths / Code / عربي* tabs and the
  Arabic word render correctly (system Arabic fallback, as in the app).
- **Camera:** every frame was checked against the intended camera. The
  sequence at 3.8 s → 13.0 s shows the stem growing frame by frame (the push
  into the leg) and the last frame of that run shows the pull-back; the
  six scenes use six different framings, so no two consecutive shots show the
  photo at the same scale.
- **Transitions:** all five scene changes are 24-frame cross-fades. Every one
  of the 1808 frames was extracted and measured (mean + standard deviation of
  the luma): **no flash frames** (the only near-uniform frames are the last
  three, where the reel deliberately fades out to paper) and **no black
  frames** (the darkest frame is the detail layer of the sharpen scene, mean
  150/255). The white-flash bug that an opaque per-scene background caused
  was found by this measurement and fixed.
- **Type:** every frame uses the app's own faces — no fallback serif appears
  in the rendered output (checked in all extracted frames).
- **Text density:** after the "no static pages" pass, no scene shows more than
  one short caption (2–6 words) plus the numbers that are the subject. The
  section headers, the stage cards and the multi-line formula cards were
  removed; what is left is a tag that rides on the picture, an arrow between
  two values, and the arithmetic one line at a time.

## 5. Limitations and honest caveats

- **No voiceover and no music.** The guide makes the voiceover the master
  timeline; no recording was supplied, and inventing one was not acceptable,
  so the reel is a caption-led explainer whose *motion* carries the timing.
  Scene timing is therefore authored, not word-aligned, and the storyboard
  timings are the source of truth. The SFX are the sounds of the movements —
  they do not narrate.
- **The SFX are synthesised, not recorded.** They are deliberately plain
  (noise sweeps, blips, ticks) so they never compete with a future
  voiceover; they can be re-voiced later without touching the timeline.
- **The reel is Arabic.** The five reference reels are Arabic-first, so the
  reel follows: the headlines are the app's own Arabic copy
  (`src/lab/arabicExplain.ts`) set in Rubik. If you want an English cut of
  the same reel, the copy is in the same files and only the strings change.
- **The style is the references', not the guide's.** The guide's default
  direction is "dark cinematic"; the five reference reels are light, warm and
  Arabic, so the reel follows them (see `docs/REEL_STYLE.md`).
- **One photo, many shots.** Every scene uses the same frame, but never at
  the same scale or angle: the ant, the leg, the stem, the head, the whole
  plant. The reel deliberately does not cut to a second photograph — the
  numbers on screen (addresses, channel values) all refer to *this* image, so
  a second photo would break the thread. The "photo changes" beats are engine
  outputs of this same photo.
- **The 3×3 kernel weights** in the sharpen scene are an illustrative
  3×3 approximation of the Gaussian the engine actually uses (σ = 1.4, radius
  5); the arithmetic shown in the maths card is the engine's real arithmetic.
- **Frame-level verification was by extraction and inspection**, not by
  watching the file on a phone; a device pass (safe areas on an actual
  Instagram/TikTok UI) is still worth doing.
- The 45 MB master can be re-encoded to a platform-friendly bitrate with the
  bundled FFmpeg if a smaller upload is needed.

## Round 4 — no orange, changing backdrops, a showreel that reads

Three notes from the owner, in order:

1. *"اللون البرتقالي مبحبهوش"* — no orange. Answered with a rule instead of a
   hue: **black, and colour only where it means something** — green for a
   result that improved, red for noise or a loss. (`good` / `bad` /
   `goodOnDark` / `badOnDark` in `src/styles/tokens.ts`; the old `accent`
   token survives only as a deprecated alias for the ink.)
2. *"بساطة ريل الـ denoise عجبتني اكتر، لكن الصورة بتفضل كتير اوي ومفيش خلفية
   ورا الانبثاقات متغيرة"* — the shots lingered and nothing moved behind the
   pops. Answered with `Backdrop` in `src/components/Chrome.tsx`: the page
   steps through four paper tones and four geometries **on every beat a scene
   hands it**, with a light band wiping the change across. The denoise scenes
   gained a beat list, more motion-sounds and real motion in the hold — the
   outlier is physically pulled out of the 3×3 window and the cell takes the
   median back, a counter of repaired pixels runs, a noisy→clean proof card
   wipes, the metrics land, the verdict stamps arrive.
3. *"شوريل مليان تعقيدات والكلام بقى متلخبط والأرقام نصها مش واضح"* — the
   showreel is cut back to **one photograph, one big number, one caption** per
   shot; the second matrix, the histogram and the stats row are gone and the
   numbers that stayed were enlarged.

### Bugs found and fixed on the way

- **`StudioReveal`'s curtain travelled the wrong way.** It started open and
  ended up covering the scene, so the denoise reel's entire first shot (the
  noisy picture, 7 seconds) was hidden behind a paper-coloured panel for
  every render so far. It now starts closed and lifts.
- **`ShotFlash` took its colour from "the accent"**, so after the palette
  change every cut flashed a full-frame green screen. It is a three-frame
  white blink now.
- The showreel's root was still the old `#08090B` black; it is paper now.
